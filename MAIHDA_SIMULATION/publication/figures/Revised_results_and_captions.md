# Revised Results passages and figure captions

## Numerical availability of frequency estimates

When affected people averaged 1.2 events, the hurdle frequency component produced acceptable fits in 1,875 of 3,000 datasets with 100 expected affected people (62.5%), compared with 2,698 of 3,000 with 500 expected affected people (89.9%). When affected people averaged three events, the corresponding figures were 2,996 of 3,000 (99.9%) and 3,000 of 3,000 (100%). Figure 2 presents the individual settings, with the total sample size shown alongside the expected number affected. Poisson and NB2 produced acceptable frequency fits in all 12,000 main datasets.

## Accuracy of frequency estimates

Figure 3 and Table 3 report mean RMSE for frequency among affected people, giving each stratum equal weight. RMSE summarises differences between estimated and true stratum frequencies; lower values indicate estimates closer to the truth under this measure. All three models within each setting were evaluated on the same datasets, restricted to those in which all three produced acceptable fits. Between 283 and 500 datasets contributed per setting.

When affected people averaged three events, hurdle had lower mean RMSE than NB2 in all 12 main settings. The paired reduction ranged from 0.255 to 0.954 events across settings. For example, at 0.5% occurrence and a total sample of 100,000 (500 expected affected people), mean RMSE without stratum interaction effects was 0.358 for hurdle and 1.057 for NB2, a paired reduction of 0.699 events (95% Monte Carlo interval 0.674-0.724). With stratum interaction effects and occurrence and frequency moving together, the corresponding RMSEs were 0.713 and 1.184, a reduction of 0.472 events (0.448-0.495). With the two components moving in opposite directions, they were 0.742 and 1.696, a reduction of 0.954 events (0.937-0.971). Poisson RMSEs in these three settings were 2.311, 2.177 and 2.372, respectively.

When affected people averaged 1.2 events, hurdle had lower mean RMSE than NB2 in the four opposite-direction settings, with paired reductions ranging from 0.063 to 0.121 events. NB2 had lower mean RMSE in the eight settings without stratum interaction effects or with the components moving together, with reductions ranging from 0.003 to 0.064 events. For example, at 0.5% occurrence and a total sample of 20,000 (100 expected affected people), RMSE without stratum interaction effects was 0.159 for hurdle and 0.105 for NB2; NB2's paired reduction was 0.054 events (95% Monte Carlo interval 0.047-0.061). In the opposite-direction setting at the same occurrence, sample size and frequency, RMSE was 0.159 for hurdle and 0.227 for NB2; hurdle's paired reduction was 0.068 events (0.063-0.073). The directions of these main hurdle-versus-NB2 comparisons were also supported under population weighting.

Figure 3 also shows changes between the two sample sizes within each occurrence level. In the opposite-direction scenario at 0.5% occurrence and an average frequency of three events, increasing the total sample from 20,000 to 100,000 reduced mean hurdle RMSE from 1.112 to 0.742 events. NB2 RMSE decreased from 1.811 to 1.696, while Poisson RMSE changed from 2.380 to 2.372. All 500 datasets contributed at both sample sizes in this example. Across all 24 main settings, hurdle had lower mean frequency RMSE than Poisson. Complete model means, common-subset sizes and paired hurdle-minus-NB2 differences are reported in Table 3.

## Figure captions

Figure 2. Numerical availability of frequency estimates across the 24 main settings. Points show the percentage of all 500 simulated datasets per setting producing an acceptable frequency fit under the prespecified data-support and numerical criteria. Panels distinguish population occurrence, frequency among affected people and the three stratum patterns. Same and opposite directions refer to how occurrence and frequency vary across strata. Horizontal-axis labels give expected numbers affected and total sample sizes. Lines connect the two tested levels; intermediate sizes were not evaluated. All models used the primary regularisation. Passing the criteria does not establish accuracy.

Figure 3. Frequency-estimation accuracy across the 24 main settings. Points show mean dataset-level RMSE, giving every stratum equal weight. Within each setting, all three models use exactly the same acceptable datasets; the number contributing is printed in each panel. Bars show approximate 95% Monte Carlo intervals for each mean RMSE, calculated from this common subset. Paired intervals for differences between models appear in Table 3 and should not be inferred from overlap of the plotted intervals. Panels and sample-size labels match Figure 2. All panels share a vertical scale. Lower RMSE means smaller estimation errors, not fewer health events.

## Notes for use

Differences and intervals were calculated before rounding. Positive reductions in this narrative are comparator RMSE minus the better-performing model's RMSE; Table 3 instead consistently reports hurdle minus NB2. Across-scenario and sample-size comparisons are descriptive; the quoted Monte Carlo intervals concern within-setting paired model differences. The 24 main settings are shown here. Keep the eight conventional-count and four very-sparse scenarios in their separate Results subsections. Keep additional regularisation and numerical-restriction sensitivity analyses separate as well.
